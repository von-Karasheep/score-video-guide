# One English cover design per work, delivered in two ratios

Create one shared cover design for the whole work, including when its movements are produced in separate sessions or delivered as separate videos. Check the work folder first and reuse its approved design. Deliver two work-level PNGs: 3840 × 2160 (16:9) and 3840 × 2880 (4:3). All movement indexes can point to that same pair. A request for one cover for all movements refers to the shared design. If only one ratio exists, keep its accepted file and compose the missing ratio. A user request explicitly limited to a particular upload slot takes the requested dimensions for that slot. Retain the editable composition in engineering. Preserve the aspect ratios of the portrait and score. Use Minion Pro for English titles and performance information, rather than indiscriminately applying the video's MiSans. Use no decorative border. Check composer, work, conductor/soloists/orchestra, and year. Use the full work identification on its cover. Movement numbers and individual tempo headings belong on the movement title pages.

Keep a coherent visual series without forcing identical portrait positions. The title must read clearly and the authentic score is background context. Do not place a tiny isolated white score rectangle on the cover without explanatory purpose; use an integrated pale real-score background. If composer and conductor are the same person, one accurate portrait is sufficient.

## Portrait identity

Prefer a clear same-performance frame or a documented historical portrait. Check face, era, clothing, posture, and visible gesture. Favor natural seated/profile/conducting poses over rigid standing. Do not generate an additional person merely for movement or substitute a remembered face for a reference.

From 2026-11-07, every cover is assembled after the people have been cut out. Do not paste a photograph, video frame, or cropped rectangle onto the layout, including a faded, bordered, or captioned still. Cut out the performer or a coherent performance group from a verified frame. Preserve the faces, gestures and relevant instruments, and remove the unrelated stage and rectangular edge. Then compose the cover from those cutouts, the verified score page, and the type.

Inspect the source frame before cutting and again after the matte. Record the source time and the cutout file. If an editor or model makes the matte, record that treatment; do not present the result as an untouched photograph. When a photographic matte cannot separate the figure, generate only that figure with the identity and posture preserved, then embed the real notation and precise type. If the likeness is wrong, replace the source frame. The video's lower-right performance window remains a real rectangular picture; this rule is for the standalone covers.

## Match the existing series

When an accepted cover or film of the same work already exists, open that film before inventing another poster. Compare the form page, one bar inside a long section, the motif card, and the cover. Sharing the layout coordinates is not the same picture.

The work title is the largest line. The composer's name stays a smaller line above it. A pale, still-readable opening page fills the background. The people are large cutouts overlapping that page. Do not make the surname the poster title, add a "score-following guide" kicker in its place, fade the score until the opening system disappears, or line rectangular stills along the bottom. For each ratio, reflow the typography and choose a readable score crop. Position the portrait so the performer's hands and baton remain clear of the lettering. Check all identifying information in both layouts. Set the type as Minion outlines in the editable source, and record that the matte was processed.

A generated figure often arrives as a JPEG on a flat color, not as an alpha PNG. Key that color, eat the fringe on hair and fingers, crop to the person, and keep the credit line clear of the hands. Keep the person's own aspect. Record the source time and that a model made the matte.

## Score background and text

Use a verified page from this work, retaining edition/page/source/hash. Reduced opacity, cropping, scaling, and layout are allowed; generated score-like texture is not a substitute. Confirm a recognizable theme, instrumentation, or body heading. A matching composer name alone is insufficient. A sample page must be identified as such; missing appropriate score imagery remains incomplete.

Check long-title line breaks in actual renders with the real local font. FontTools outlines can prevent export substitution. Size text using actual glyph widths. Use the work title as the primary line, with composer, key and opus, performers, place and date, followed by the smaller edition and actual maker credit. A standalone single-movement work keeps its own title. Include only information justified by the task. Avoid unsupported edition/master/losslessness claims.

## Preserve and inspect

Keep original reference images, source-frame time, prompts and generated assets where applicable, score page/hash, font paths, editable cover source, and both work-level PNGs. Copy project assets into durable project storage rather than relying solely on temporary paths.

Inspect both finished covers at full size and thumbnail size: dimensions, uncut lettering, identifiable person, cutouts rather than rectangular inserts, accurate identity/version, authentic score, spelling, and thumbnail readability. Link both completed sizes. Show the new size when supplying a missing one. Preserve prior versions when revising.

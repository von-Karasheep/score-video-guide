### Thematic and tonal analysis required in every subsequent movement

User correction, 2026-11-10, from the Dvorak 7/I revision:

- The body header's upper-right region is a persistent first-theme/second-theme or work-appropriate motif legend. Do not replace it with maker credit. Put genuine maker/revision credit on opening/end pages and covers instead. Keep identities and colors consistent in the legend, motif card, analytical text and timeline; distinguish rehearsal letters from thematic labels.
- For a sonata movement, explain the first and second thematic groups, transition and closing material where relevant, development, abbreviated or transformed reprises, and coda. The analysis must follow the conflict and return of tonal centers as well as thematic transformations. Generic remarks such as "the orchestration thickens" or "tension increases" are insufficient by themselves. Returning fragments do not automatically mark the start of the recapitulation.
- Every analytical card has a concise tonal-status line and a score-based explanation in the right panel, between prose and motif. At an actual tonal change, explain the previous/new center and evidence: bass/pedal, chord pitches, leading-tone resolutions, cadential arrival and relevant signature/accidentals. A key signature alone does not determine key; distinguish stable key, tonicization, chromatic sequence and an isolated major/minor chord. Account for transposing instruments. If the passage is modulatory, say so rather than invent one fixed key.
- Explain how tonal opposition is resolved or remains unresolved in this specific work. A tonic-major second-theme reprise does not imply a final major-key ending, nor does tonic return automatically mean emotional triumph. Attribute interpretive disagreements; do not apply a textbook sonata template mechanically.
- Left-align main form headings at their start ticks. Subsidiary theme and event labels, printed measure ranges and output start times follow the same adopted playback order as the cards and overview. Use elapsed time for widths and retain permanent ticks. Document pickup and repeat conventions.
- Research the music before freezing captions: combine the actual score with substantive work-specific analysis, scholarly literature where available, and suitable original orchestra/composer/performer notes. Read the relevant body rather than counting search hits or citing unread titles. Keep reading scope, editions, specific supported claims and conflicts in the source ledger; favor formal DOI/publisher links and label theses/webpages correctly.
- Explain visible notation briefly at relevant occurrences: boxed rehearsal letters (location aids, not keys or themes), a2 (both players on the part), Solo, instrumental transposition labels, dynamics/accents/hairpins, trills, articulation and tempo changes as applicable. Interpret symbols in context; a wind trill and a timpani roll are different actions. Callouts last long enough to read and do not cover notation.
- Actively seek names for every featured principal or exposed soloist, including flute, oboe and cello; once verified, show the name rather than stopping at the instrument. Use the score's precise part names throughout: distinguish `第一小提琴` and `第二小提琴` when the pictured section is established, and preserve other supported numbered/divided parts. A waiver for one unresolved player is not a general waiver of this research requirement. See the 2026-11-11 shot-caption correction in layout-and-render.md; it applies to future production and explicitly does not reopen the completed Dvořák movies. Use exact-event written credits or explicit user identification of the pictured player; store the evidence per shot. Never identify a face or infer a historical player solely from present personnel lists. An associated interview establishes association only, not the seat played. If the requested name remains unresolved after a bounded search, ask for the missing identity and continue independent edits; do not guess or silently declare the naming request complete.

These are part of the portable Bruckner/Firebird content floor. Their musical content cannot be replaced by attractive typography, a completed encode or claims of precise synchronization.

### Practical synchronization, not precision certification

The user explicitly accepts a few isolated local timing differences. A slightly early/late cursor or page change is tolerable when the viewer can still follow the same musical passage and the map recovers at the next reliable landmark. It is not tolerable for errors to accumulate across pages, recur through a substantial part of the work, show the wrong thematic section, skip/repeat music unintentionally, or finish the score while the ending is still unfolding. Missing pages, clipped motif staves and repeated label collisions are layout/content failures, not permitted timing imprecision.

Use performance-derived measure anchors, automatic alignment candidates when useful, and local manual corrections. Reserve exact-note, frame and numerical-accuracy targets for an explicitly requested precision audit. The default complete guide includes a supported current-beat display in addition to the measure, meter and playhead. Follow [layout and rendering](layout-and-render.md#left-panel-beat-display-without-information-loss) for placement and timing. If supported beat timing is unavailable, apply the blocker rule and report the missing evidence.

Before committing to full export, inspect the opening, main thematic entries/transitions, changes of tempo/meter, page turns, holds and ending, plus a small distributed sample (normally 8–12 points). Audit route/page order and monotonic timing across the full data. These are usability checks, not exhaustive attack-by-attack or every-frame certification. If checks show repeated drift or uncertain correspondence that cannot be corrected directly, apply the stop protocol below. If they pass at this standard, finish; do not expand into full-note audits or extra method comparisons merely to seek perfection. Record actual checks and any localized exceptions outside the film. Classify as `complete_score_guide` / `approximate_measure_sync`; reserve `precision_approved` for separately requested and actually verified work. A successful default deliverable does not need an additional “approve the simplification” question.

## Source selection and the three different comparisons

1. Search for **lossless audio and watermark-free pictures of the performance the user supplied**. They may come from different uploads of that same event. Verify work/version, conductor, orchestra, soloists if any, event date, venue/occasion, and release/broadcast information using source descriptions and credible event/release records. Record links and agreements/conflicts. Matching only a conductor, orchestra and year is insufficient when several events fit. Unknown identity is a blocker, not permission to assume.
2. Once event identity is adequately established from information, do not perform audio fingerprinting, waveform correlation, exhaustive frame matching or cross-upload sample comparisons merely to prove “same performance.” The user explicitly does not require them. If sources have different introductions/edits, use a small number of identifiable musical/visual landmarks to set the actual audio-picture offset and segment cuts. Stop and report unresolved edits; do not turn the task into forensic matching.
3. **Export fidelity is different:** compare the chosen source audio interval to the finished output PCM to verify preservation and title/end silence. This remains a technical check, not a cross-source identity test and not proof of musical cursor accuracy. Preserve source precision; do not copy Firebird's historical truncation as policy.

For another movement from the same upload and part, reuse the retained original streams after checking identifiers, metadata and hashes. Keep any analysis WAV or feature cache separate from the delivery input. Record the source range and exact output sample bounds for each movement. The Rome FLAC inputs were stereo, 48 kHz and 24 bit. Their PCM was carried in ALAC and compared across the entire retained music interval. Apply the actual source format and precision to each new performance.

If lossless audio, clean same-event pictures, or sufficient event information cannot be obtained through a bounded search, state exactly what is available and what is missing, propose the fallback, and wait for the user's choice. No silent switch to another performance, lossy audio, or a watermark-removal crop.

## Rendering checks learned from the later Dvorak movements (2026-11-11)

Before a full encode, check character coverage in the actual body and subtitle fonts, including flat, sharp and natural signs (♭, ♯, ♮). A valid string or a successful render does not prove that a glyph is visible: inspect the resulting tonal text. Keep the accepted MiSans typography; use a verified, bundled symbol fallback where MiSans lacks a glyph, and include that fallback and its license in the portable project. Subtitle rendering must also see the fallback.

Build instrument labels from each page's actual visible staves. Never carry a fixed orchestral row count into another movement or a divided-staff page. In this score, movement III normally has 13 rows and omits trombones, but divided flute or cello pages have 14; movement IV normally has 15 and divided flute pages have 16. These counts are edition-specific examples, not universal orchestral rules. Recheck motif row indices when an excerpt crosses such a page. Preserve every required voice.

Inspect real motif crops at the opening and changed instrumentation. Retain complete notes, articulations and meaningful expression words such as legato or Solo; do not cut their upper edge. A notation explanation must match the actual printed word. Correcting a crop or missing glyph only reopens the affected visual checks, not an already adopted practical timing map.

A relocated project must rebuild from relative, fingerprinted frozen inputs without reading the finished movie. Preserve the selected background, concert crop, exact audio, dynamic overlay, required fonts and compatible encoder, plus the command and editable score/analysis data. A convenience symlink in the old working directory is not a replacement for standalone inputs. Record whether the delivered full export used those exact inputs and whether a separate second encode was actually run; do not claim the latter from a script inspection.

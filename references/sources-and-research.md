# Sources and research

Read [production-policy.md](production-policy.md), especially its distinction between event identity, media offset and export PCM fidelity.

## Recording identity and actual quality

Record the specified URL or local file first. Check performers, orchestra, soloists, date, venue/occasion, and release identity against official orchestra, label, or video-release information where possible. Inspect the actual container, video codec/resolution/frame rate, audio codec/rate/channels/verified bit depth, bitrate, duration, opening, applause, decay, and closing material. An extension or download badge does not prove readability or losslessness. Report DRM restrictions without bypassing them.

Search for lossless audio and watermark-free footage of the exact event supplied by the user. Information-based event confirmation is sufficient; no cross-upload frame-by-frame/audio-fingerprint verification is required. If no suitable lossless/clean same-event source is found in a bounded search, report the options and wait before accepting a fallback. Do not change performances merely to obtain a lossless format. A Bilibili quality label is not a codec or bitrate measurement. If only AAC is obtainable, say so; conversion to ALAC/FLAC preserves decoded PCM through later processing but cannot restore the master. Even a server-provided FLAC does not establish that earlier production was never lossy.

Keep the original and make separate outputs. Stream-copy original audio when compatible. Sample-accurate trimming or silent title/end pages may require lossless encoding. Do not upsample merely to advertise a larger rate.

## Score suitability

Prefer a complete, verifiable score matching the performance: the user's suitable copy; publisher/composer/orchestra archives or a trusted public music library; then another accessible source checked against its title/copyright pages, instrumentation, body, page count, and critical passages. Samples, parts, reductions, or arrangements support only their covered scope.

Distinguish original/revised/editorial versions, reprint and publication dates, performance dates, orchestral/stage/reduction/arranged forces, and an analysis score versus the actual conductor's copy. A composer conducting does not resolve cuts, repeats, or edition differences. Identify the score actually used for analysis without inventing evidence about the score used at the concert.

## Acquisition and score inspection

- Search for the score before asking the user where it is. On 2026-11-08 the user required this for every later work: look through the machine, concert folders, downloads, and cloud drives, then publisher catalogs and public music libraries, and download a complete file when one is obtainable. A missing file in the concert folder is not a reason to stop and wait.
- Download accessible materials; record login, purchase, or copyright limits only after that search. Finding a catalog, a sales page, or a copyright lock is not obtaining the body.
- Inspect cover, first music page, transitions, and last page; then the relevant full scope. Check missing pages, skew, spreads, OCR contamination, and cropped instrument labels.
- Retain original SHA-256. Derived cleaning/cropping/deskewing must not remove notation, clefs, meters, barlines, or evidence.
- Keep printed pages, PDF indices, systems, printed measures, and repeated performance occurrences distinct.
- Never generate replacement notation or missing score pages with an image model. Use score frames extracted from a score-video source only if the user accepts it and identity, completeness, and readability are verified.

## Evidence for analysis

Prefer published DOI/publisher links. Label preprints, theses, reports, and webpages accurately. An abstract or contents page is not a read full text.

Use the score for pitches, meter, orchestration, dynamics, repeats, and divisions; published analysis for formal/motivic/harmonic interpretations; composer statements and official programs for history and titled sections; release records for performance identity; this recording for actual times and acoustic events. Literature can be wrong: resolve conflicts against score and sound rather than copying a claim solely because it appears in a paper.

For each used source, retain author/title/year/publication/pages/DOI or URL/type, the pages actually read, supported claims, and limitations. Separate a theme, motif, accompaniment pattern, and interval cell. Attribute an analyst's particular naming.

Write analysis around what to listen for, which voice carries it, what changes, and how it relates to previous material. Do not substitute terminology or mood labels for audible specifics. Share section/motif identities among timeline, cards, and score annotations. Choose the form appropriate to the work, not a universal sonata template.

Place brief references near claims and readable references after the music. Supply full clickable bibliography and reading scope separately. Exclude search results that did not inform the final analysis.

## Music and timing

Use the [shared gesture masters and per-video archive](conducting-gestures.md) for every conducting card. The printed meter describes the score; the chosen master describes the way this conductor groups it.

1. Map the route through the printed edition in actual playing order. Give each performance occurrence its own time, including a pickup, repeated exposition, alternate endings, returns or a transition into the next movement. Keep printed measures and printed pages identifiable. Map the cursor and score change to these occurrences. Start the cursor at the first sounding note position, account for local tempo and fermatas, and preserve the last release.
2. Display meter, written beat unit and current count. Set the gesture unit by watching and listening to this performance. Align clearly visible downbeats, rebounds or restarts with the orchestral attacks in their musical context. Across instrument shots interpolate between checked anchors at the local tempo. If the anchors fall on either side of a tempo change, use an audible intermediate landing. Record anchor source/time and distinguish observed gestures from estimated intervals in the production notes. At a fermata hold the baton and resume on the actual cue. Include lead-in, final release and decay.
3. Op. 60/II and III both use 3/4. The Rome Adagio subdivides each quarter into two. In the fast scherzo one gesture spans a bar while the written three beats remain readable. The scherzo and trio alternate A–B–A–B–A. In IV the exposition is played twice with alternate endings, and the last three fermatas need separate holds and restarts. Recheck these choices for every new work and performance.
4. The footer uses two levels and cumulative elapsed progress. Above the band, bracket each large formal span once and left-align its heading at that span's opening tick, with the small inset in the layout data. Examples include `呈示部`, `展开部`, `再现部` and `尾声`. Below the band, identify the theme or event, printed measure range and **output-video** start time. Keep the future pale, fill the current span from its left edge and leave completed spans filled. Give performed repeats separate spans with the returning theme's color. Check that headings stay at the opening ticks in the encoded frame. Abbreviate, stagger or combine secondary labels if needed to keep their text clear, retaining the complete cue list in the index.

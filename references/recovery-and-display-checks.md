# Recovery, MIDI comparison, and display checks

See [Reuse-first tool selection](tool-selection.md) for stage-specific software and documented-but-untested candidates.

## Reuse before invention; immediate assessment and wait

Inspect the project and installed skills first. For an unavailable capability or a failed existing method, search GitHub for skills/tools and Google or available web search for plugins and official documentation before building replacements. Save query, URL/revision, capability, local test outcome, and reason for selection/rejection. A wrapper around the same engine is not an independent method. Do not repeatedly rebuild an OMR engine, alignment engine, or encoder to avoid looking for a maintained implementation.

Follow the [blocker rule](production-policy.md#reuse-tools-and-stop-promptly-on-a-real-blocker) for a failed feasibility pilot or uncertain recovery. The no-MIDI comparison routes below are optional diagnostics for a specific need.

## When no matching MIDI exists

1. Search for matching MusicXML/MXL/MIDI, verifying edition, instrumentation, performed route and completeness. MIDI is symbolic events, not the orchestra's recording. Retain the specified performance as delivery audio.
2. Pilot the same short, readable passages across distinct routes. Known tools: [Audiveris](https://github.com/Audiveris/audiveris) and [HOMR](https://github.com/liebharc/homr) for score-to-MusicXML candidates; [Basic Pitch](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch) for audio-to-MIDI candidates. The [audio-to-midi skill](https://github.com/maystudios/claude-skills/blob/main/audio-to-midi/SKILL.md) is a Basic Pitch workflow, not a fourth engine. Official repositories were rechecked on 2026-11-09; inspect compatibility and revisions again before installation. Do not execute an external skill's installer blindly.
3. On identical score crops compare raw OMR, corrected clef/key metadata, and shorter system/staff crops retaining the real score header. Record failed exports as failures. Check pitches (including transposition), rests, ties, tuplets, durations, bar boundaries and voice assignment against the image. Never stretch wrong durations merely to fill the meter.
4. Compare transcription of the specified recording with score-derived events and separate spectral/onset diagnostics. Audio MIDI preserves recorded seconds but may include room noise, applause, octave errors and overlapping voices. A container tempo such as 120 BPM does not establish the performed tempo or meter. Fourier bins are not uniquely separated instruments. Basic Pitch's single-instrument suitability does not establish orchestral transcription accuracy.
5. Keep independent raw outputs and an event comparison table: source crop/time, method/version, expected note and position, prediction, difference, resolution and evidence. Use separate held-out passages after tuning. Agreement between two unverified algorithms is candidate agreement, not ground truth; do not vote errors into approval.
6. Correct accepted score events, preserve provenance, export MIDI plus MusicXML/event data and a tempo/route map as appropriate. Missing/uncertain fields remain explicit. Use reviewed landmarks to map the MIDI to actual performed measures; rebuild page/beat/cursor timings from that map. Do not feed raw full-recording transcription directly into the score cursor. Apply the stop rule if the pilot cannot establish a usable route.

Barber case evidence: the project under `预习视频/工程文件/巴伯第一交响曲-法兰克福广播-codex/同步/精确同步修订/多方法对比/` contains `对比报告.md`, `omr-comparison.json`, `split-comparison.json`, and Basic Pitch outputs. Audiveris, HOMR and Basic Pitch were actually run, with failures as well as partial results. ScanScore, PlayScore 2 and Oemer were researched but not tested there; Matchmaker is alignment, not an independent score recognizer. These case results are not universal accuracy guarantees or a required local-path dependency.

## Display failures and their repairs

| Failure | Required repair | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Nearby hues make adjacent form sections look like one span | Follow the [form timeline rules](layout-and-render.md#timeline-opening-and-references) and keep visible dividers over the fill. | Inspect the pale and filled tracks at playback size before and after each transition. |
| Line stalls during timpani/sustained passage | In measure-linear mode discard internal event holds and collapsed x knots; sweep the bar using its real start/end. Initial silence still waits. | Three equally spaced frames inside the same bar have equal x increments within raster rounding; no motionless sounding bar. |
| Small jump at each barline | End one interval and begin the next at the same boundary/time on the same system. | Compare consecutive encoded frames around boundaries; allow clean new-system cuts. |
| Missing Chinese instruments | Map each actual staff row per page/system, including omitted instruments, divisi, harp's two staves and percussion changes. | Overlay y markers on original left-margin crops; review every page's order, then check final text. |
| Cloud input repeatedly stalls | Cache once locally, verify identity and reuse it for encode/PCM checks. | Save hash and path; do not reference a cache deleted after the previous export. |
| Requested half-second cut desynchronizes labels | Apply one explicit edit map to audio, PiP, pages, beats, motifs, callouts and timeline. | Verify retained PCM independently by sample-range removal, and check video PTS/frame-grid phase. |

Staff-label data needs page, system, source image dimensions/crop, staff y, Chinese instrument name and original abbreviation. Do not assume all pages have the first page's instrumentation. Preserve printed player numbers when unreadable rather than guessing them. Draw labels in left whitespace using the same transform as the score; keep clefs, key signatures and existing abbreviations. Check minimum row spacing, text bounds and no overlap. Barber's 97-page/2310-row map is an example, not a universal template.

## First-occurrence notation explanations

Before freezing the render data, compare every displayed score page or system with the source edition. Scan above and below all staves, including inner parts, the bottom staff and the rightmost measures. Cover meaningful tempo, dynamics, expression, articulation and technique marks, including common dynamics such as `p`, small text such as `cresc.` and `dim.`, and stacked combinations. Preserve every printed mark in the crop. Build a notation registry with a meaning ID, exact glyph or text, staff/page/measure, source bounding box, verified Chinese explanation, first performed occurrence, output start/end and callout geometry. Resolve any unclear mark against the source and reliable documentation.

Treat stacked instructions as a single entry with the full printed wording and combined meaning. The 2026-11-19 feedback includes `p` (“弱奏”), `cresc.` (“渐强”), `dim.` (“渐弱”), `p cresc.` (“从弱奏开始渐强”), `p dolce` (“弱奏，音色柔美”) and `p sempre` (“始终保持弱奏”). Register a combination even if one of its components has already been explained. Group simultaneous instances of the same instruction across parts when a shared callout can still point clearly to the relevant staff or staves. Apply the same timing and placement checks to technique marks such as `pizz.` (pluck the strings), `arco` (return to bowing) and fermatas (prolong the indicated note or rest).

At the first relevant performed occurrence, show the printed mark and its short Chinese explanation for about 1 s, with longer display time as needed for readability. Use vacant space and a short leader ending beside the exact mark when necessary. Keep the callout clear of the score, staff labels, cursor, motif, analysis and performance inset. Queue simultaneous new marks when needed to keep their score associations clear. Explain an instruction again when its musical meaning changes. Use the performed onset for timing even when the page is visible earlier. Transform the highlight and leader coordinates with the score crop and scale, and remap times after any edit.

Register directions written out in full, including those above the top staff and between staves. In the Op. 125 scherzo, `Ritmo di tre battute` and `Ritmo di quattro battute` require separate callouts at their respective grouping changes (“每三小节一组” and “每四小节一组”). The grouping changes over the written 3/4 meter. If a grouping direction is printed beside `sempre pp`, explain the grouping and the complete dynamic (“始终保持很弱”) as distinct instructions. A callout for `pp` alone does not cover `sempre pp`. Check successive playing-technique changes in each affected part. Explain `pizz.` and the later `arco` where the relevant strings switch to plucking and back to bowing. Use the full direction in the card, wrapping long wording so the original and the Chinese explanation remain legible.

Before delivery, check the encoded video at each scheduled callout against the registry. Verify that the original engraving is intact and readable, the complete mark and Chinese explanation are visible while the instruction applies, and any outline or leader points to the correct staff and mark. Check onset, mid-display and end frames and the relevant page turns for clipping, occlusion and carryover onto the wrong page. Resolve missing or unreadable callouts before completion.
